Buying a home in the Netherlands

Dutch mortgage rules are strict and the paperwork is unforgiving. This page explains how we work, what it costs and which risks we calculate for you. We advise in English; your written advice report is in Dutch, with an English summary on request.

What matters here

Three things that surprise people from abroad

Your contract type counts

A permanent contract makes everything easier. With a temporary contract, an employer's statement of intent or a so-called perspective declaration can still make it work.

  • Employer's statement in a fixed national format
  • Probation period usually blocks an application
  • Self-employed: we need an independent income assessment
  • Income from abroad limits your choice of lender

Conditions in the purchase contract

Dutch purchase agreements are binding quickly. The financing condition is your protection, and it has a hard deadline.

  • Without it you owe 10% of the price if financing fails
  • A bank guarantee or deposit is usually required
  • Extending the deadline must be requested in time
  • We tell you that deadline in writing and remind you once, a week before — watching the date stays with you

The property itself

Not every home is financed on the same terms, and some are barely financed at all.

  • Ground lease (erfpacht) affects your costs and options
  • Apartments: the owners' association is assessed
  • Older homes: foundation problems occur in some regions
  • Energy label affects how much you can borrow

Risks

We calculate first, then talk about insurance

Insurance is not an answer to a risk you do not know you have. So we start by calculating what happens to your income in six situations, and whether you could still pay your housing costs.

  • Incapacity for work — our standard scenario is 50%, calculated both with and without finding suitable work
  • Death — can the surviving partner stay in the home, and do they want to?
  • Unemployment — how long can you bridge a drop in income?
  • Separation — can one of you carry the home alone?
  • Interest rate rise after your fixed-rate period ends
  • Retirement — if the mortgage runs past your state pension date
We calculate using what you actually have left to live on each month, not an average household. If nothing is missing, our advice is to do nothing — that is advice too.
After signing

Statutory aftercare is free

We inform you about material changes to your product or its conditions at no cost: that is our legal duty and we do not sell it as a service. Beyond that we do not monitor your mortgage, and we will not warn you when your fixed-rate period ends: keep that date yourself. A subscription is a discount arrangement, not a monitoring service: you pay a fixed amount per month and pay less for whatever you ask us to do.

Please note

What our figures do and do not say

  • Net monthly costs are an indication. You pay the gross amount to the lender. What you get back through your tax return depends on your income, the value of your home, your assets and that year's tax rules, so it differs per year and per person. You cannot rely on a net figure.
  • How long you still have mortgage interest relief is an estimate. Relief runs for a maximum of thirty years per home loan, and that clock carries over from earlier years in which you had relief. We work with what you tell us about your housing history; we have no access to your tax records and cannot check it.
  • We are not tax advisers. We do not determine your tax position, we do not file or check your tax return, and we do not advise on box 3, gifts or inheritance. Interest on the part of the loan you spend on something other than your own home is not deductible. For certainty, ask the Belastingdienst or a tax adviser.
  • Buying together with unequal contributions? Who bears which part of the debt is recorded at the notary, not with us. Towards the lender you are both liable for the whole debt, and that is what our calculations assume.
Complaints and withdrawal

If you are not satisfied, or change your mind

  • Tell us first. We confirm your complaint within five working days and reply in writing, with reasons, within four weeks. If a claim for damages is involved we report it to our professional indemnity insurer, which can extend that period; we will tell you if it does.
  • Then Kifid. If we cannot resolve it together, you can take your complaint to Kifid, the Dutch complaints institute for financial services, where we are registered under number 999.999999. Do so within three months of our final position, and in any case within one year of filing the complaint with us. You can always go to court instead.
  • Fourteen days to withdraw. Did you enter into the agreement at a distance — by email, by phone or with a digital signature — then as a consumer you have fourteen days to withdraw without giving a reason, counting from the day after the agreement was made. If you ask us to start during that period and withdraw afterwards, you pay pro rata for the work done.
  • The full procedure is in the Dutch documents: the service guide, the assignment agreement and the general terms. Ask us and we will walk you through them in English.
